Over a century of warming

Atlantic sea surface temperatures have risen about 0.9 °C since 1900, with the fastest increases happening in recent decades.

SSP1-2.6

Low emissions

If the world cuts emissions aggressively, Atlantic SSTs stabilise close to today's levels — roughly +0.4 °C more by 2100.

SSP2-4.5

Moderate emissions

A middle-of-the-road path. Temperatures keep rising but more slowly, reaching about +1.1 °C above today by 2100.

SSP5-8.5

High emissions

If fossil fuel use accelerates, the Atlantic could be 2.5 °C warmer than today by 2100 — well above anything in the historical record.

All scenarios

The gap is a choice

Nearly 2 °C separates the best and worst cases by 2100. That difference is determined by the emissions decisions made over the next few decades.

Final Project

Hurricanes, climate, and rising ocean temperatures

So far we have been brainstorming ideas for our final project. We’ve landed on the idea of telling a story about how hurricanes are connected to sea temperature and precipitation, and how climate change may intensify these factors and lead to stronger hurricanes and greater destruction in affected communities.

Our prototype website currently includes one of the visualizations we plan to use: a line chart of Atlantic Ocean temperatures with future climate projections that are revealed as the user scrolls. We are also working on developing additional visualizations that will help communicate the relationship between climate change and hurricane activity more clearly.

We expect the most challenging part of the project to be creating interactive and engaging hurricane visualizations. While we have found strong examples for visualizing individual hurricanes, we want to focus on showing long-term trends to emphasize how hurricanes correlate with environmental factors such as ocean temperature and precipitation. Our goal is to create visualizations that not only present data, but also tell a compelling story about increasing hurricane destruction and the climate conditions that may contribute to it.
